A FIELD GUIDE

Make your thinking visible.

You do not need a perfect UML diagram or runnable code. You need enough detail for someone to follow a decision through your objects.

  1. Set the boundaries.
    State assumptions and what your design leaves out.
  2. Assign the decisions.
    Name objects, their responsibilities, and how they collaborate.
  3. Follow the behaviour.
    Walk through a success and a failure, including state changes.
  4. Explain the compromise.
    Describe an alternative and a test that could challenge your design.
Feedback is a review checklist, not a grade. Text checks locate phrases; they cannot establish that your design is correct. Different designs can be equally valid.
